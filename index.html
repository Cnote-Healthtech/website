What you get

Structured, coded data from every clinical statement

Every result passes an independent verification step before it is released, and its confidence is calibrated, so a score of 90 means right about nine times in ten.

Coded output

A SNOMED CT concept for every statement; ICD-10 (UK) with OPCS-4, or ICD-10-CM (US); and the UMLS CUI.

Broad coverage

13 core semantic types, 16 report-specialised types across radiology, pathology, cytology, endoscopy, cardiology, respiratory, neurophysiology, nuclear medicine and genomics, plus your own custom types.

Calibrated confidence

Two calibrated scores per statement: the probability it is correct, and the probability it is correct or debatable.

Independent verification

Each statement is re-checked against its source text and low-scoring diagnoses are retried before anything is released.

Full traceability

Every result carries its document, section and position, a readable sentence restating it, and a provenance stamp: SNOMED CT release, calibrator version and settings.

Self-hosted

Runs on a single GPU server. Two model sizes: Large, with calibrated confidence, and Medium, for GPUs with less memory. Whole episodes of care are processed document by document.

How it works

Five layers, from document to coded episode

  1. 1

    Chunking

    Splits each document into sections and recognises tables, lists and structured blocks.

  2. 2

    Extraction

    A two-pass extractor finds each clinical statement, then fills its type-specific details. Tables and structured content are parsed deterministically.

  3. 3

    Normalisation

    Three biomedical encoders and a learned reranker propose SNOMED CT candidates; a language model selects the concept. Medications are resolved against RxNorm, and ICD-10 / OPCS-4 codes follow from the official maps.

  4. 4

    Verification

    Each statement is re-checked against its source text, low-scoring diagnoses are retried, and a trained calibrator turns the evidence into a 0–100 confidence.

  5. 5

    Coding episodes

    Optionally assembles the verified statements into a coded episode of care.

Evidence

Finds more documented conditions than professional coders

On real hospital admissions C-NOTE found more of the documented conditions than professional clinical coders did, with comparable accuracy.

+14 ptslead on the expert annotators' conditions (95% CI +10 to +18)
+12 ptslead on the hospital coders' conditions (95% CI +7 to +17)

How this was measured

The 61 hospital admissions of the published MDACE Inpatient test set (MIMIC-III, 616 expert-annotated diagnoses) carry two independent sets of human codes: the MDACE expert annotators' evidence-linked ICD-10-CM codes and the hospital's original clinical coding.

Identical codes or concepts matched automatically. Every other difference was put to a panel of large language models (GPT-5.6 and DeepSeek-V4-Pro, with Claude Opus 5 settling their disagreements) with passages from the notes, under the same rules for C-NOTE and for both human sets.

Accuracy is comparable: 90% of C-NOTE's conditions were correct in every detail, against 92% for the MDACE annotators and 86% for the hospital coders. C-NOTE was never trained on MIMIC-III.

The verdicts are model-adjudicated, not reviewed by a person.

What C-NOTE extracts

Every statement carries five universal modifiers: polarity, temporality, certainty, experiencer and conditionality†. Its main concept is always coded to SNOMED CT.

Disease

  • diagnostic status† affirmed, suspected, ruled out, absent, resolved
  • body sites*
  • laterality*†
  • TNM stage
  • tumour grade
  • invasive size
  • residual cancer burden
  • histology subtype
  • metastatic sites*† each coded as its own secondary-neoplasm concept

Symptom

  • status† present, denied, resolved, improving, worsening, historical, anticipated
  • severity*†
  • body sites*
  • laterality*†

Finding

  • laterality*†

Oncology detail

* value maps to a SNOMED CT concept (body structure or qualifier value). † returned as structured data: a fixed value set, a number with its unit, or a list. Report-specialised types (for example imaging finding, pathology tumour, biomarker, genomic variant) add report-specific fields such as breast density or margin status.

Deployment

Built to be configured. Output you can use directly.

Adapts without retraining

  • Custom semantic types in a short YAML file: their parameters and value sets, whether they are coded to SNOMED CT and ICD-10 or OPCS-4, which report types and sections they apply to, and a few worked examples. They then flow through extraction, coding and output like the built-in types.
  • Scoped settings by document type, section or semantic type: switch types on or off, deduplicate at document or visit level, and turn code mapping on or off.
  • Restrict or prioritise concepts with SNOMED CT Expression Constraint Language to match a registry or value set.
  • UK or US code system: ICD-10 with OPCS-4, or ICD-10-CM.
  • Your hardware, your thresholds: pick the model size that fits, and set your own confidence thresholds per use.

Loads straight into your warehouse

  • Relational tables, one per semantic type, with numeric values and units in their own linked table, ready for a warehouse, registry or research database.
  • Every row carries the SNOMED CT, ICD-10 and OPCS-4 codes, both confidence scores, its source document and section, and the verification decision.
  • Every run records the terminology release, calibrator version and settings that produced it.
