C-NOTE · CNOTE Healthtech

Clinical text to SNOMED CT, verified and confidence-scored

C-NOTE reads clinical documents and turns every clinically meaningful statement into structured, coded data: a SNOMED CT concept, the details that qualify it (negation, timing, certainty, laterality, severity, body site, dose and more), ICD-10 and OPCS-4 codes where they apply, and a calibrated confidence score.

Every result passes an independent verification step before it is released. It runs on a single GPU server inside your own network, so patient text never has to leave it.

How it works

  1. 1

    Chunking

    Splits each document into sections and recognises tables, lists and structured blocks.

  2. 2

    Extraction

    A two-pass extractor finds each clinical statement, then fills its type-specific details. Tables and structured content are parsed deterministically.

  3. 3

    Normalisation

    Three biomedical encoders and a learned reranker propose SNOMED CT candidates; a language model selects the concept. Medications are resolved against RxNorm, and ICD-10 / OPCS-4 codes follow from the official maps.

  4. 4

    Verification

    Each statement is re-checked against its source text, low-scoring diagnoses are retried, and a trained calibrator turns the evidence into a 0–100 confidence.

  5. 5

    Coding episodes

    Optionally assembles the verified statements into a coded episode of care.

Evaluation against clinical coders

On real hospital admissions C-NOTE found more of the documented conditions than professional clinical coders did, with comparable accuracy.

How this was measured

The 61 hospital admissions of the published MDACE Inpatient test set (MIMIC-III, 616 expert-annotated diagnoses) carry two independent sets of human codes: the MDACE expert annotators' evidence-linked ICD-10-CM codes and the hospital's original clinical coding.

Identical codes or concepts matched automatically. Every other difference was put to a panel of large language models (GPT-5.6 and DeepSeek-V4-Pro, with Claude Opus 5 settling their disagreements) with passages from the notes, under the same rules for C-NOTE and for both human sets.

C-NOTE's lead is +14 points (95% CI +10 to +18) on the expert annotators' conditions and +12 points (+7 to +17) on the hospital coders' conditions. Accuracy is comparable: 90% of C-NOTE's conditions were correct in every detail, against 92% for the MDACE annotators and 86% for the hospital coders.

C-NOTE was never trained on MIMIC-III. The verdicts are model-adjudicated, not reviewed by a person.

Confidence threshold

Each statement carries a calibrated confidence from 0 to 100, and one threshold trades recall for precision: for example a recall-favoured setting for coder assistance and a stricter one for automated feeds.

ThresholdPrecisionRecallKept
0 keep everything90%95%100%
40 coder aid94%90%80%
85 automated96%79%56%
9598%61%32%

A condition is kept when any statement supporting it reaches the threshold. Precision is the share of kept conditions judged correct as documented; recall is the share of the expert-annotated conditions documented in the notes that C-NOTE still finds. Measured on both MDACE sets (87 admissions) with the same model-adjudicated labels as above. Raising the threshold from 0 to 85 lifts precision from 90% to 96% and cuts the list to review by 44%, at the cost of 16 points of recall.

Configuration and output

Built to be configured

C-NOTE adapts to your documents and your downstream use without retraining.

  • New semantic types can be defined in a short YAML file: their parameters and value sets, whether they are coded to SNOMED CT and ICD-10 or OPCS-4, which report types and sections they apply to, and a few worked examples. They then flow through extraction, coding and output like the built-in types.
  • Settings can be scoped by document type, section or semantic type: switch types on or off, deduplicate at document or visit level, and turn code mapping on or off.
  • Concepts can be restricted or prioritised with SNOMED CT Expression Constraint Language to match a registry or value set.
  • Choose the UK (ICD-10 with OPCS-4) or US (ICD-10-CM) code system, pick the model size that fits your hardware, and set your own confidence thresholds.

Output you can use directly

Results are written to relational tables, one per semantic type, with numeric values and units in their own linked table, so they load straight into a warehouse, registry or research database.

  • Each row carries the SNOMED CT, ICD-10 and OPCS-4 codes, both confidence scores, its source document and section, and the verification decision.
  • Every run records the terminology release, calibrator version and settings that produced it.
